RESPONSIBLE NEWS RECOMMENDATION
Persuasion-Aware, Knowledge-Graph-Augmented Recommendation for Trustworthy News Delivery
Aligned with UN Sustainable Development Goal 16: Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions
Project Report
3 August 2026
Executive Summary
Responsible news recommendation aims to bridge personalized news delivery with content trustworthiness, aligning with SDG16 (Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions). This project proposes a novel system that detects persuasive techniques (e.g. fear appeals, authority appeals) in news through NLP, builds user persuasion profiles, and integrates them into a knowledge-graph-augmented Graph Neural Network (GNN) to rank news for individual users.
The proposed Persuasion-Aware News Recommender uses the MIND and SemEval-2023 datasets, supplemented with Clickbait, GoEmotions and BASIL corpora, to train BERT-based persuasion detectors alongside a GNN over a news graph enriched with entities. A Responsible News Score is designed to balance relevance against persuasion risk. Evaluation covers accuracy (AUC, nDCG@K), Persuasion Exposure Reduction, fairness, and explainability. The contribution is a detailed architecture, methodology and benchmark suite: to our knowledge, the first system to combine persuasion detection with graph-based news recommendation.
1. Introduction
The scale and velocity of online news distribution have made personalized recommendation indispensable for helping readers navigate daily information flows. However, recommender systems that optimize purely for engagement metrics such as click-through rate can inadvertently amplify sensational, emotionally charged, or manipulative content, since such content tends to generate stronger short-term engagement signals.
This creates a tension between two goals that are often treated separately in the literature: delivering content that is relevant to a user's interests, and delivering content that is trustworthy, balanced and free of manipulative framing. Left unaddressed, this tension can erode public trust in information institutions and contribute to polarization, which directly undermines the aims of United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 16 (SDG16): promoting peaceful, inclusive societies and accountable institutions.
This project introduces a Persuasion-Aware News Recommendation framework that explicitly models persuasive language at the content level and factors this signal into the ranking process, alongside conventional relevance modelling. The system combines transformer-based persuasion detection, knowledge-graph construction over news entities, and graph neural network based user-item modelling, culminating in a Responsible News Score that governs final rankings.
2. Problem Statement
SDG16 calls for peaceful, inclusive societies and accountable institutions. A central challenge to this goal is the flood of news and misinformation that undermines public trust and social stability. Biased or sensational news can escalate conflict and polarize societies, working against SDG16 objectives.
The core research question addressed in this project is: how can a news recommender system simultaneously engage the user and curtail their exposure to manipulative content? This project addresses the question through three concrete objectives:
Detect persuasive content (e.g. fear appeals, appeals to authority, loaded language) in news articles and flag high-risk pieces at the sentence level.
Build user profiles capturing susceptibility to specific persuasion techniques, and use these profiles to reduce exposure to matching content in the user's feed.
Employ explainable and fair ranking models to preserve transparency and maintain user trust in the recommendations produced.
Responsible news recommendation is therefore framed as the task of delivering timely, relevant news while upholding transparency, fairness and factual integrity, in direct support of SDG16's targets for trustworthy information and inclusive public dialogue.

Recent news recommender models such as HieRec, MINER and NRMS focus on user interest modelling and may incorporate topical or sentiment signals. Graph-based methods such as GLORY and SentiEntityRec capture higher-order relationships via GNNs, but none of the reviewed systems explicitly address persuasive manipulation. SemEval-2023 provides the first large-scale dataset annotated for persuasion techniques, and independent studies confirm that persuasion features are useful for detecting disinformation in isolation, yet none of these features have been incorporated into recommendation systems directly.
Existing responsible-recommendation work, such as algorithmic nudging studies and provider-fairness models, controls exposure either through post-hoc interventions or fairness constraints, without leveraging content-level persuasion analysis. Three gaps emerge consistently across the literature:
Persuasion-aware personalization: no existing system modulates recommendations based on an analysis of persuasive language in the candidate content.
Responsible ranking objectives: most recommender systems optimize purely for relevance or engagement, not for reduced exposure to manipulative content.
Explainability and fairness: few models provide content-level explanations or explicit fairness guarantees across news categories or sources.
This project addresses these three gaps by integrating persuasion detection with graph-based recommendation and by introducing new evaluation metrics, including Persuasion Exposure Reduction, tailored to measuring responsibility in news recommendation.
4. Proposed Methodology
The proposed Persuasion-Aware News Recommender combines NLP and graph learning modules in an end-to-end pipeline, illustrated in Figure 1 and described component-by-component below.
4.1 Data Inputs and Preprocessing
The primary data inputs are the MIND news corpus, user click logs, and SemEval-2023 persuasion annotations. Preprocessing extracts text, metadata and named entities from each news article, and links entities to a reference knowledge graph such as DBpedia or Wikidata. Sentences and paragraphs are labelled with persuasion categories using a fine-tuned BERT-based persuasion classifier trained on SemEval-2023 Task 3 data.
4.2 Persuasion Detector
A transformer model (RoBERTa or BERT) is trained to detect 23 persuasion techniques, including fear appeals and appeals to authority, at the paragraph level. Applied to MIND articles, it outputs a persuasion-score vector per news item. These scores are aggregated across a user's click history to form a Persuasion Profile representing the distribution of persuasion types the user has been exposed to.
4.3 Knowledge Graph Construction
A News Graph is constructed with nodes representing users, news articles, topics and knowledge entities such as people, locations and concepts. Edges capture relations including user-to-clicked-news, news-to-entity and news-to-topic links, extending prior knowledge-graph-based approaches such as DKN with richer semantic structure.
4.4 GNN Encoder
A Graph Neural Network, such as GraphSAGE or a Graph Convolutional Network, is applied over the combined news-entity-user graph. News nodes carry features derived from title and body embeddings and entity embeddings; the Persuasion Profile is fused in as an additional user-side feature. The GNN produces user and news embeddings that jointly reflect content interests and persuasion-awareness.
4.5 Responsible Ranking Function
A Responsible News Score is defined for a candidate news item n and user u as a weighted combination of relevance and persuasion risk:
Score(u, n) = α · Relevance(u, n) − β · PersuasionRisk(u, n)
Relevance(u, n) is computed from the similarity of GNN embeddings, while PersuasionRisk is a function of the persuasive techniques detected in n, scaled by the user's persuasion susceptibility profile. The trade-off weights α and β are tunable, and fairness adjustments (e.g. filtering to avoid over-representation of any single source) may be incorporated into the scoring function.
4.6 Model Training
The GNN is trained to predict user clicks via binary cross-entropy loss, with an optional auxiliary adversarial loss for provider fairness. The Persuasion Detector is trained separately on SemEval and related persuasion-technique datasets through BERT fine-tuning. The overall system uses multitask learning, jointly optimizing click prediction and persuasion-exposure minimization, with a combined loss of click log-loss plus a lambda-weighted persuasion penalty.
4.7 Evaluation Metrics
Accuracy metrics: AUC, nDCG@K and Precision@K for click prediction.
Persuasion Exposure Reduction: the reduction in average persuasion score of recommended news relative to a baseline recommender.
Fairness metrics: parity in click rate across news providers or topics, e.g. Precision@K measured per category.
Explainability: interpretability scores reflecting how clearly a recommendation can be attributed to specific entities or persuasion types in the user's profile.
The experimental protocol uses MIND with SemEval labels mapped onto articles via source matching, with users split temporally into training, validation and test sets. The Persuasion BERT model is fine-tuned on SemEval data for multi-label paragraph classification, and the GNN is trained using graph propagation in PyTorch Geometric. Baselines for comparison include a standard GNN news recommender (GLORY), a semantic-only recommender (NRMS), and a fairness-only model (ProFairRec), with all metrics reported using significance testing across repeated runs.

5.1 Deployment and Scalability
Offline vs. online processing: a bulk offline phase constructs and updates the global news knowledge graph on a weekly cadence, with graph embeddings recomputed accordingly. The Persuasion Detector runs as a preprocessing step on newly ingested articles, while each user's persuasion profile is updated incrementally after every session.
Real-time serving: for a logged-in user, the system fetches their profile and recent clicks, queries the GNN for candidate scores, and applies the Responsible Score formula for reranking. Feature caching and approximate nearest-neighbor retrieval are used to keep latency low.
Scalability: the knowledge graph can be sharded by topic or time window, and mini-batch GNN training methods such as GraphSAINT support training over large-scale graphs. Cloud infrastructure (e.g. managed ML services) supports horizontal scaling of individual components.
Knowledge graph updates: new articles and entities are incorporated through an incremental construction pipeline, using entity-linking tools such as DBpedia Spotlight or spaCy to attach new nodes.
Privacy and federation: following approaches such as FedNRM, user models can be partially trained on-device via federated learning, sharing only aggregated gradients. Differential privacy noise can optionally be added when updating persuasion profiles to protect individual user data.
Explainability and logging: intermediate recommendation explanations (for example, noting that an item was recommended for matching a topical interest while avoiding a specific persuasion technique) are logged to support accountability, and the pipeline is kept modular so each component can be evaluated independently.

7. Expected Outcomes and Novelty
The proposed framework is novel in explicitly combining persuasion detection with personalized recommendation. The expected contributions of this project are:
Persuasion-awareness: a fine-grained persuasion detection module, beyond simple sentiment or bias classification, incorporated directly into the recommendation loop. To the best of current knowledge, no prior work uses persuasion signals such as fear or authority appeals to filter or re-rank news recommendations.
An integrated GNN model: a unified graph model fusing user interests, news content (entities and topics), and persuasion profiles, enabling responsible rankings that penalize manipulative content for susceptible users. While GNNs have previously been used for news recommendation, this GNN is unique in simultaneously ingesting persuasion features and knowledge-graph context.
A Responsible Ranking Formula: an explicit Responsible News Score balancing relevance and persuasion risk, a novel objective relative to existing systems that optimize for accuracy or fairness in isolation.
An evaluation framework: new metrics, including Persuasion Exposure Reduction, combined with fairness and explainability measures tailored to responsible news recommendation, adapting ideas from fairness-aware recommendation to content-level analysis.
SDG16 alignment: by design, the system addresses misinformation and manipulation, supporting SDG16's goals of inclusive access to factual information and accountable media, with analysis expected to demonstrate that reduced persuasion exposure corresponds to more balanced content consumption.
In summary, this project is expected to deliver: (a) the first end-to-end Persuasion-Aware News Recommender; (b) a thorough evaluation on standard benchmark datasets using new SDG-aligned metrics; and (c) insights into how personalization can be guided by content-ethics criteria without materially sacrificing recommendation relevance.
